The honest hunt for a Sharpe-3 stock-picking strategy

Eight hours of systematic research on the repo's point-in-time data — S&P 500 & NASDAQ-100 PIT panels, the 24,000-ticker delisting-inclusive Tiingo universe, 1M SEC 8-K filings, and intraday data. Everything net of costs, next-day execution, walk-forward.

Verdict

A durable, honest, out-of-sample, net-of-cost Sharpe 3 is not attainable from this data — and the arithmetic says why, not just the search. The only holding period with enough breadth to reach 3 even theoretically is one day (ceiling 3.07, with zero costs assumed); there the cost drag alone is −1.95 Sharpe. Every horizon that can pay its costs has a theoretical ceiling below 0.7. Realized: the a-priori four-sleeve ensemble makes OOS +0.22, with a bootstrap CI containing zero.

This mirrors the repo's earlier crypto verdict (crypto_pulse/research/SHARPE3_VERDICT.md) — different asset class, same three walls.

06Better construction? Tested, and it adds nothing

Every portfolio above is a naive decile sort, carrying large uncontrolled factor bets — so the obvious suspicion is that a risk model was the missing piece. I built one: rolling PCA on the trailing 252-day member correlation matrix, refit monthly, applied forward only, then factor-neutral inverse-vol optimized weights.

The risk model does its job — 70% of portfolio volatility removed. But gross Sharpe moves only 0.62 → 0.71: the factor exposure was carrying risk, not concealing alpha. And with the same cost drag measured against a third of the volatility, net Sharpe gets worse. Construction cannot manufacture information.

07The horizon trap — the wall, quantified

This is the decisive result. Sharpegross = IC × √breadth, and breadth = Neff × (252/h). The S&P 500 cross-section measures Neff = 315 (464 names, mean residual correlation 0.001) — the breadth genuinely is there. Measured IC per horizon against what survives costs:

The trap has two jaws. Only the 1-day horizon has a theoretical ceiling above 3 — and only barely (3.07), assuming a perfect risk model, optimal weights and zero costs. There, turnover is 864×/year and the cost drag alone is −1.95 Sharpe units. The horizons whose costs are affordable (21d, 63d) have theoretical ceilings of 0.69 and 0.36 — unreachable even with free trading and perfect execution.

Reaching Sharpe 3 at a tradeable 5-day horizon needs IC ≈ 0.024 (2× the best measured); at 21 days, IC ≈ 0.049 (4.4×). Nothing across 24 families of price, volume, event, filing, fundamental or ML signal came within a factor of two.

08The three walls

1 · The information wall Measured rank-IC of everything engineerable from this data — 24 families, a 34-feature walk-forward ML in two configurations, and an IC-weighted combination of all of it — sits at 0.007–0.017 (t ≈ 2–3.6) at every horizon from 1 to 63 days. The walk-forward combination of all ten headline signals scores IC 0.0076: combining does not raise it, because the signals are different projections of the same thin information.
2 · The decay wall Every classical anomaly reproduces in-sample exactly where the literature found it, and every one is ≈ 0 or negative after 2018 in liquid US equities. The yearly tables show the institutionalization of short-horizon stat-arb end-to-end.
3 · The cost wall The horizon trap above. Separately, where gross edges do persist (small caps below $20M ADV, gross 0.6–1.2), realistic 10–20 bps costs produce net −3 to −8: the legendary "Sharpe 3–5 small-cap reversal" is bid-ask bounce — the same mechanism this repo already documented in crypto, reproduced here in equities.

What a real Sharpe 3 requires — all outside this dataset: per-stock intraday data with an execution simulator, options/borrow data, or true non-price information (news text, transcripts, alt-data). The audited strategies in this repo (GRANITE-XL ≈ 0.9, SUMMIT ≈ 1, PULSE ≈ 1.2–1.5) sit at the honest ceiling for infrastructure-free research.
