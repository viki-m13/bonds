AEGIS all-weather leveraged risk parity

A monthly DCA across 3× NASDAQ (TQQQ), 3× long Treasury (TMF) and 2× gold (UGL) — inverse-vol weighted so each carries equal risk, with a continuous momentum tilt toward whichever is trending, vol-targeted to 22%. It is built for one job: survive & win the deep crises. It beats QQQ-DCA through the dot-com crash (+21%) and the GFC (+21%) at a third-to-half the drawdown — and it honestly trails in tech bull markets. This is crisis armor, not a QQQ-beater.

Read this first — the honest trade-off. Over the full 2000–2026 sample, DCA-ing into plain QQQ ends with more money (AEGIS is of QQQ-DCA) because that window was dominated by a historic tech bull, and no diversified strategy beats a concentrated tech bet in a tech decade. AEGIS earns its keep in the opposite regime: when tech craters, its 3× Treasury / gold sleeves rally and it pulls ahead. Own it if you want deep-crisis protection or don’t want to bet everything on tech — not if you simply want the most dollars in a rising market. Its companion VOLT is the opposite bet.

How it works

the mechanism

Risk parity. Each sleeve is sized by 1/its volatility, so 3× NASDAQ, 3× Treasury and 2× gold each contribute roughly equal risk — no single asset dominates.

Momentum tilt. A continuous tilt (e3z) leans toward whichever sleeve is trending: into tech in bull markets, into Treasuries/gold when tech breaks and they rally. Smooth, not a binary switch — which is why it survives rebalance-day robustness where switches don’t.

Vol target. The whole basket is scaled to 22% annual vol; the remainder sits in cash. This caps drawdowns and is why AEGIS is ~ cash today.

Read this before trusting it

  • It trails in bull markets — by design. Full-sample it ends at of QQQ-DCA. Diversifying away from tech is pure drag when tech leads (the 2010s: 0.70×). You are buying crisis protection, and it is not free.
  • It is a regime bet, not alpha. AEGIS wins when tech crashes and Treasuries/gold rally (deflationary busts: dot-com, GFC). In a bust where bonds also fall (2022) its edge shrinks to +11%; in fast shallow shocks (2018, COVID-V) it can lag. No diversifier wins every crisis.
  • Long-Treasury (TMF) before 2009 is reconstructed from FRED constant-maturity yields (duration/convexity calibrated to real TLT 2005–2018: 0.95 daily-return correlation, slightly conservative). TQQQ pre-2010 and UGL pre-2008 likewise reconstructed from real underlyings.
  • Headline is the best rebalance day. Full-sample ratio across trade days is — consistently below 1, i.e. it robustly trails QQQ-DCA on raw wealth on every day, and robustly wins the crises on every day.
  • Leverage & taxes. Triple-leveraged ETFs carry daily-decay and can fall >30% in a month; monthly rebalancing generates short-term gains (best in a tax-advantaged account). Backtest is pre-tax, 10bps/side.
