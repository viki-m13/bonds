Systematic Strategy · Factsheet

VOLT vol-targeted leveraged NASDAQ

A monthly dollar-cost-average that scales exposure to 3× NASDAQ (TQQQ) by its own volatility, holding a 50/50 gold–Treasury sleeve for the rest, with a volatility-acceleration overlay that de-risks faster into crashes and a reversal dial that leans back in when NASDAQ is oversold but its long-term trend is intact. It beats DCA-into-QQQ on every rebalance day (~1.7–2.7× the money, 2006–26) — a risk-managed leverage dial, not stock-picking alpha.

How it works & how drawdowns are managed

the whole mechanism
1 · Vol-targeting

Each month, set the TQQQ weight to 30% ÷ TQQQ’s own volatility. Calm markets → ~50–65% in 3× NASDAQ; when volatility spikes in a selloff the weight falls automatically toward zero. Exposure is largest when risk is low, smallest when risk is high — no forecasting.

2 · Acceleration overlay

Volatility level lags a fast crash, so when the 20-day vol jumps above the 63-day vol the estimate is inflated by (fast÷slow)² — de-risking faster into an accelerating drop and not re-levering until it calms. This cut the dot-com drawdown −65% → −59%.

3 · Reversal dial

Pure vol-targeting de-levers into the bottom — volatility peaks exactly when price is cheapest, so it sells low. The dial leans weight back up when NASDAQ is oversold (below its 50-day MA) but its secular trend is intact (above the 200-day MA), to catch the rebound. A broken long-term trend (dot-com) disables it, so it never levers into a real collapse.

4 · Defensive sleeve

Whatever isn’t in TQQQ sits in a 50/50 gold + long-Treasury blend — not cash. Gold held up in 2022 when stocks and bonds fell together, so the sleeve carries genuine crisis insurance.

5 · The result

Together these hold the drawdown near plain QQQ’s (−45%; −59% incl. dot-com) instead of buy-and-hold TQQQ’s −94% — while compounding at 24.5%/yr. Managing the downside is the return engine: avoided leveraged losses compound.

6 · Honest robustness

The headline is the month-end figure. Rebalanced on any other day of the month the edge is 1.7–2.7× QQQ-DCA (2006–26) / 2.0–3.3× (1999–26) — it beats QQQ-DCA on every trade day, so it is not calendar luck, but a fair central estimate is ~2×, not the 2.7× peak.

Dot-com stress test

1999–2026 · reconstructed TQQQ · cash defense

QQQ trades back to 1999, so VOLT can be run through the worst tech crash on record. Buy-and-hold 3× TQQQ lost −99.9% in 2000–2002 and never recovered (2% CAGR over 27 years). VOLT’s vol-targeting cut TQQQ exposure to 14–27% as volatility spiked to 108–208%, and the acceleration overlay trims the dot-com drawdown further — from −65% to −59%. The reversal dial stays switched off through the crash (QQQ was below its 200-day MA), so it never adds leverage into the collapse.

VOLT QQQ-DCA
$1,000/mo · 1999–2006

Read this before trusting it

  • It is leverage, not alpha. The excess return is mostly compensation for holding ~1.6× average NASDAQ exposure. Its Sharpe (0.95) is only marginally above plain QQQ’s (0.90) — essentially the same risk efficiency; you get more absolute return by taking more risk, not a large free lunch. And it only outperforms in a tech-led world — it is a leveraged bet that NASDAQ keeps leading.
  • The headline is the best rebalance day. Month-end gives ~2.7× QQQ-DCA (2006–26); other trade days give 1.7–2.7×. It beats QQQ-DCA on every day tested, but budget for the lower end, not the peak.
  • Plan for a −59% drawdown, not −45%. The −45% shown above is the 2006–2026 window; run through the dot-com crash (2000) the true max drawdown is −59% (the vol-acceleration overlay cut it from −65%) — most from the first leg down before even the fast vol window had risen. Vol-targeting bounds the damage (buy-and-hold 3× TQQQ was −99.9%) but does not eliminate it. You must be able to hold through more than half your capital.
  • Leveraged series pre-2010 are reconstructed from real underlyings (TQQQ launched 2010). Validated at 0.999 daily-return correlation vs real TQQQ, and slightly conservative. On real TQQQ (2011–26) the edge holds.
  • Tail the vol window can’t dodge: a fast overnight gap-down before trailing volatility has risen is the one risk the 63-day estimate cannot de-risk ahead of.
  • Taxes & turnover: monthly rebalancing generates short-term gains — best held in a tax-advantaged account. Backtest is pre-tax, 10bps/side.
