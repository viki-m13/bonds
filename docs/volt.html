Dot-com stress test

1999–2026 · reconstructed TQQQ · cash defense

QQQ trades back to 1999, so VOLT can be run through the worst tech crash on record. Buy-and-hold 3× TQQQ lost −99.9% in 2000–2002 and never recovered (2% CAGR over 27 years). VOLT’s vol-targeting cut TQQQ exposure to 14–27% as volatility spiked to 108–208%, and the acceleration overlay trims the dot-com drawdown further — from −65% to −58%.

Read this before trusting it

  • It is leverage, not alpha. The excess return is compensation for holding ~1.6× average NASDAQ exposure. Its Sharpe (0.84) is below plain QQQ (0.90) — you get more absolute return at slightly worse risk efficiency, not free outperformance.
  • Plan for a −58% drawdown, not −44%. The −44% shown above is the 2006–2026 window; run through the dot-com crash (2000) the true max drawdown is −58% (the vol-acceleration overlay cut it from −65%) — most from the first leg down before even the fast vol window had risen. Vol-targeting bounds the damage (buy-and-hold 3× TQQQ was −99.9%) but does not eliminate it. You must be able to hold through more than half your capital.
  • Leveraged series pre-2010 are reconstructed from real underlyings (TQQQ launched 2010). Validated at 0.999 daily-return correlation vs real TQQQ, and slightly conservative. On real TQQQ (2011–26) the edge holds.
  • Tail the vol window can’t dodge: a fast overnight gap-down before trailing volatility has risen is the one risk the 63-day estimate cannot de-risk ahead of.
  • Taxes & turnover: monthly rebalancing generates short-term gains — best held in a tax-advantaged account. Backtest is pre-tax, 10bps/side.
