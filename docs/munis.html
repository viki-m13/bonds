1Executive summary

Individual municipal bonds cannot be day-traded profitably — the dealer bid–ask spread eats every short round trip. But that same dealer-market friction creates a durable, harvestable inefficiency for a patient systematic buyer: when a customer is forced to sell a bond well below its own recent level, a rules-based strategy that buys the dislocation and holds it to reversion earns a statistically significant excess return over random entry in the same bonds.

The strategy — buy when a bond prints ≥3 points below its own trailing 60-day median, hold ~1 year, sell into a customer bid — was validated on a purpose-built harness designed to make self-deception hard: fills only at prices real customers actually received, a matched random-entry control that nets out carry and bond selection, a pre-registered parameter grid, and a locked out-of-sample test. It is a mean-reversion / liquidity- provision strategy and carries the corresponding risk: it underperforms in a sustained rate selloff (2022). We disclose that failure mode prominently rather than optimize it away.

What a manager is buying

A repeatable, capacity-aware source of +3.25% per-trade excess return over a matched control (p<0.001) in a $4-trillion, structurally inefficient asset class where the incumbent liquidity provider (the dealer) captures the spread — here re-routed to the patient investor. Signal, execution model, and full backtest are reproducible from public data.

2The opportunity & microstructure

The US municipal market is roughly $4 trillion across ~1 million CUSIPs and ~50,000 issuers. Unlike equities, there is no central limit order book: bonds trade over-the-counter through dealers, most CUSIPs trade only intermittently, and retail investors buy at the dealer's ask and sell at the dealer's bid. Every trade is reported to the MSRB and republished on EMMA, giving a complete regulatory tape of prices, yields, sizes and sides.

Why naive timing fails

The median same-day customer round-trip spread in our data is ~0.44 points (mean ~0.9). A two-week trade earns ~0.2% of carry — it cannot cover a ~0.9-point spread. Short-horizon reversion and momentum signals all lose and none beats a random-entry control (§6).

Why patience wins

The spread is a one-time cost; carry plus pull-to-par is a growing return. Past ~6 months the average customer round-trip turns positive; by one year it is materially positive. The dislocation signal then adds selection on top of that base.

4Methodology — the honesty harness

The rules below were fixed before results were produced. Their purpose is to make a false positive hard to manufacture.

  • Fills only at real customer prices. Buys fill at the day's par-weighted customer-buy print (retail band $5k–$250k); sells at the customer-sell print. Never mid, never inter-dealer — a customer cannot trade there. If the required side did not print, there is no trade.
  • No intraday assumptions. EMMA timestamps are date-granular; a signal on day t enters at day t+1's prints at the earliest.
  • Trailing-liquidity gate, no lookahead. A bond is tradable only if it printed on ≥8 distinct days in the trailing 90.
  • Coupon accrual credited (muni prices are clean).
  • Matched random-entry control. Every strategy is scored against random entries in the same bonds, same window, same exit logic and cost model. Excess-vs-control — not raw return — is the headline metric: it removes carry, spread and bond selection, isolating timing skill. Significance is a 2,000-sample cluster bootstrap.
  • Pre-registered grid + locked OOS. Families and parameter grids were specified in advance; entries ≤2022-12-31 are in-sample; one config per family is locked by a fixed rule and evaluated once on 2023-01-01→2026-07-07.

5The signal

KEYSTONE entry rule

Enter when a bond's customer-buy price prints ≥3.0 points below its trailing 60-day median mid (a forced-seller dislocation) and the bond clears the liquidity gate. Fill at the next customer-buy print. Exit at the first customer-sell print after a 365-day minimum hold (hard stop 455 days). Coupon accrues throughout.

The signal is deliberately own-history and price-based. A yield-based version is confounded by market-wide rate moves and adds little once the control nets out bond identity (§6). A price dislocation relative to the bond's own recent level cleanly captures idiosyncratic forced selling — fund redemptions, tax-loss selling, estate liquidations — which mean-reverts.

8Risk, regime & capacity

Known failure mode: sustained rate selloffs

KEYSTONE is short volatility / short a rate shock. In 2022, muni yields repriced up for a full year; "3 points below the 60-day median" then fired on every bond as the whole market fell, with no reversion — the strategy bought falling knives (−1.99% excess, 32% win). This is intrinsic to mean-reversion, not a fixable bug. Size it accordingly.

Regime overlay (optional)

Gating entries on the broad market (only buy when MUB ≥ its 100-day average) removes most of the 2022 loss and lifts full-sample excess to ~+4.3%, but cuts trade count ~6×. We present it as a risk overlay, not baked into the core — filtering on the very event that motivated it invites curve-fitting, so both versions are disclosed.

Capacity & positioning

Deep idiosyncratic dislocations are rare — on the order of ~230 per year in the sampled book, out of ~7,000 bonds trading ≥250×/yr nationally — and each fill means being the buyer into someone else's forced sale. Coverage scales with the tape: the full EMMA universe is ~500k CUSIPs, so the opportunity set is far larger than the sampled backtest. This is a capacity-limited, patient-capital strategy, complementary to a core muni book, not a high-turnover sleeve. As of the last data date it is dormant: no bond trades ≥3 points below its own trend, exactly as a liquidity-provision strategy should behave in calm markets.

9Honest limitations

  • Out-of-sample window overlaps a muni bull market. Yields peaked late 2022 and fell through 2024, flattering the OOS (+4.5%) vs in-sample (+2.1%). The full-sample +3.1% excess is the honest headline; treat +2% as the conservative through-cycle estimate.
  • Equity-curve drawdown is understated. Because individual munis do not print daily, each trade's realized return is spread geometrically across its holding period, smoothing intra-trade volatility. Total return and CAGR are real; the path is optimistic — true mark-to-market drawdown is deeper.
  • Universe survivorship. EMMA's discovery endpoint lists only bonds that traded in the sampling window, so earlier years over-represent survivors. The strategy trades healthy bonds at mild (~3pt) discounts where this bias is small; it explicitly does not attempt deep-distressed ("buy at 60") names, where survivorship would be fatal without default data.
  • Execution assumes a fill at the observed customer-buy print. Realistic for patient capital taking the other side of forced selling, but a live book must source inventory and may not always fill at the modeled level.
  • Backtest trades a liquid sample, not the whole market. Discovery now spans all 54 states/territories (~500k CUSIPs); the backtest runs on ~3,100 of the most liquid/seasoned bonds. Extending to the deeper liquidity tail is an engineering scale-up, not a methodology change, and only enlarges the opportunity set.

10Implementation & reproduction

The entire pipeline — data acquisition, validation, panel construction, backtest, config lock, out-of-sample run, and this document's data — is reproducible from public sources:

python scripts/build_universe.py # discover the tradable universe (EMMA) python scripts/download_trades.py # full per-CUSIP trade histories python scripts/validate_data.py # structural + cross-endpoint validation python research/panel.py # daily per-bond panel python research/run_backtest.py is # in-sample grid python research/lock_configs.py # mechanical config lock python research/run_backtest.py oos # one-shot out-of-sample python research/current_picks.py # today's live screen

Deployment for a live book: run current_picks.py daily against the fresh EMMA tape; entries surface only when forced selling creates a ≥3-point dislocation in a liquidity-eligible bond. Position sizing, credit screening, and an optional MUB-trend overlay sit on top of the raw signal.
