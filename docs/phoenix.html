How it works — in plain English.

Seven different traders. Seven different signals. One equal-weighted portfolio. One tail-risk brake. No borrowing, no margin, no shorting.

Instead of picking one “best” way to trade, PHOENIX runs seven different trading strategies at the same time and blends them in equal parts. Each uses a different kind of signal — three follow trends in different ways, one buys sharp dips, one trades a calendar effect, one follows the bond market, one follows Bitcoin and Ether. Honesty requires saying the quiet part out loud: the three trend-followers often win and lose together. Most of the smoothing comes from the other four.

Three of the seven are variations of the same trend bet. The diversification is real — but modest, not magic. Why seven, not just one

Blended together, some of the ups and downs cancel and the ride is smoother than most single sleeves — though not dramatically so. On top of that, one simple “brake” halves exposure automatically in the rare weeks when market volatility hits extremes.

Why seven, not just one?▾

Every single one of these strategies has years where it loses money. But an honest caveat first: this page used to claim the sleeves “almost never lose on the same days” and had near-zero correlations. That was a measurement artifact — the sleeves' return series were misaligned in time, which made them look independent when they weren't. Measured correctly, the three trend sleeves (VANGUARD, ORION, HELIOS) correlate 0.5–0.7 with each other: they are one trend bet expressed three ways, and bad trend months hit all three at once. The genuine diversifiers are BONDTREND, REVERSAL, TURN-OF-MONTH, and CRYPTO, whose correlations to the rest run from slightly negative to about 0.4. Diversification is real and it does smooth the ride — but it is modest, not magic.

The one safety brake (and the two we removed)▾

v4 has exactly one automatic overlay:

  • Volatility regime gate. If the portfolio's 60-day volatility breaches the top 1% of its trailing year (measured with a 2-day lag — no look-ahead), exposure is cut in half until it calms down. Very rare, but when it fires, it fires hard.

Earlier versions of this page advertised two more brakes, and honesty requires explaining where they went. The 15% vol target was removed because, under the no-margin 100% cap, it can only ever reduce exposure — it was pure return drag with no crash protection the gate didn't already provide. The drawdown throttle was removed because a sign bug made the advertised formula inert (it almost never engaged), and the corrected version made results worse out-of-sample. The strategy never uses borrowed money or leverage above 100% — only the leverage built into the LETFs themselves.

What “LETF” means and why we use them▾

A leveraged ETF magnifies the daily return of an index. TQQQ aims for 3× the daily return of NASDAQ-100. More bang per buck when right; bigger losses when wrong — which is exactly why we need good timing signals and safety brakes. Universe: U.S. stocks (UPRO, TQQQ, SSO, QLD), sectors (SOXL, TECL, FAS), real estate (DRN), international (EDC, YINN), commodities (UCO, UGL, NUGT), long bonds (TMF, UBT, TYD). Plus IBIT (Bitcoin) and BIL (cash).

How often it trades▾

A signal is generated every evening (after close, Mon–Fri). On most days there are 0–3 small trades. Annual turnover ~15×. Active but not high-frequency — one person can execute 2–3 market-on-open orders by hand each morning.

The honest risk picture▾

No strategy is guaranteed. PHOENIX can lose money. In the backtest (2014–2026), the worst peak-to-trough drop was about −17.9% — better than the S&P 500's historical −30 to −50% bear-market losses, but still meaningful, and a live drawdown can always be worse than the backtest's. An earlier claim that a synthetic 2008 stress test lost only −1.3% has been retired along with the flawed synthetic data behind it. Zero borrowed money — gross exposure is capped at 100% at every layer, with de-risked money parked in T-bills.

Where the signal comes from each day▾

After U.S. close, an automated process pulls the latest prices for the ETFs and macro data (VIX, credit spreads, rates). It runs each of the seven strategies, blends them at equal weight, applies the tail gate, and produces the trade list above. You place those trades at tomorrow's open. That's it.

N° 05 The architecture

Strategy overview & rebalancing rules.

The technical version: weights, signals, the tail gate, execution. Nothing magical — everything mechanical, deterministic, and look-ahead-free.

Overview

PHOENIX blends 7 rule-based LETF trading strategies — each using different signals — at static equal weight. Seven sub-strategies — VANGUARD, ORION, HELIOS, CRYPTO, REVERSAL, TURN-OF-MONTH, BONDTREND — each get 1/7 of the book (1/6 before 2024-01-11, when CRYPTO activates at IBIT's listing). Nothing is fitted: there are zero estimated allocation parameters. The single overlay is a vol-regime tail gate that halves exposure when 60-day realized vol breaches its trailing 252-day 99th percentile (computed with a 2-day lag). Gross exposure is capped at 100% at every layer — no margin, no borrowing.

Read the full overview & sleeve summary▾

Full-window (2014–2026, net of costs): Sharpe 1.08, CAGR 22.2%, MDD −17.9%, Calmar 1.24, Sortino 1.39. The 2019–2026 window: Sharpe 1.09, CAGR 24.8% (SPY over the same window: 0.86 / 13.8% / −32% MDD). $10,000 invested at the start of 2014 becomes about $121,000 by mid-2026 (12.1×, no margin).

The seven sleeves (equal-weighted at 14.3% each, structurally different signals):

  • VANGUARD (14.3%) — monthly rotation across QLD/UGL/TMF/TYD: 189-day momentum + 200-day-MA check, inverse-vol sizing, macro trigger ladder. Gross capped at 1.0.
  • ORION (14.3%) — weekly top-4 of 12 risk LETFs by 252-day momentum, remainder in a safe book. Wednesdays.
  • HELIOS (14.3%) — 42/189-day trend on unleveraged underlyings, expressed via LETFs. Weekly Fridays.
  • CRYPTO (14.3%) — weekly 63-day momentum on BTC/ETH via IBIT/ETHA. Active from 2024-01-11 (IBIT listing); no pre-ETF crypto is counted.
  • REVERSAL (14.3%) — 5-day dip-buys: QQQ/SPY/SMH z-score < −1 → TQQQ/UPRO/SOXL.
  • TURN-OF-MONTH (14.3%) — TQQQ over the last 4 + first 3 sessions of each month.
  • BONDTREND (14.3%) — TLT 63-day momentum: long TMF or flat.
  • QUANTUM — retired. The old ML sleeve's published history was in-sample XGBoost output; see Test 06 below.
Where the diversification really comes from (and where it doesn't)▾

An earlier version of this page claimed the sleeves were “essentially uncorrelated (max pairwise |ρ| = 0.19)” and invoked a √N Sharpe uplift. Both claims were artifacts of misaligned return dating and are retracted. Measured correctly, the three trend sleeves — VANGUARD, ORION, HELIOS — correlate 0.5–0.7 with each other: one trend bet expressed three ways. The genuine diversifiers are BONDTREND, REVERSAL, TURN-OF-MONTH, and CRYPTO (pairwise correlations from −0.19 to roughly 0.4). The blend's Sharpe (~1.08) sits above the typical single sleeve, so diversification earns its keep — but modestly, not by a √N miracle.

One tail-risk brake (two were removed)▾

The vol regime gate cuts exposure to 50% when 60-day vol breaches its trailing 252-day 99th percentile, computed with a 2-day shift — no look-ahead. That is the only overlay in v4. The 15% vol target was removed: under the hard 100% gross cap it could only subtract exposure and acted as pure drag. The drawdown throttle was removed: a sign bug made the shipped formula inert, and the corrected version hurt out-of-sample. Both removals are documented in alt/PHOENIX_V4_REVIEW.md.

Validation summary · 7 robustness tests▾

Survivorship bias, transaction-cost sensitivity, weight perturbation (2,000 MC), the 1/N-vs-allocator comparison, block-bootstrap null (10,000 iter, p < 0.0001 full-sample), the QUANTUM retirement ablation, and a synthetic-extension test that was itself retired. Full-sample Sharpe 90% CI: [0.68, 1.50]; OOS lower bound 0.58. Honest live expectation: below the ~1.1 backtest — structure-level selection bias (this is the configuration that survived three audits) is disclosed, not modeled.
