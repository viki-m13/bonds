The strategy

Buy when a bond prints ≥3 pts below its own trailing 60-day median (a forced-seller dislocation) and it clears a trailing-liquidity gate. Fill at the next customer-buy print. Hold ~1 year; exit into a customer-sell print. Coupon accrues throughout.

The dealer bid–ask spread (~0.44 pt round-trip) kills short-term timing but is a one-time cost against growing carry + reversion. Buying idiosyncratic dislocations and holding to reversion beats random entry in the same bonds — pure timing alpha, net of spread, carry and selection.

KEYSTONE-XL — the validated upgrade

The execution stack proven on corporates transfers verbatim: limit entry (fill only if buy price ≤ prior mid + 0.25), 1 position/issuer, recovery exit (first sell print ≥21d once mid recovers to entry-day median). Full window 2012–26: +7.82% CAGR at −4.8% maxDD (base +4.35% / −9.1%); OOS 2023–26 +9.29% CAGR, 98% win, excess +2.37% (p<0.001) vs a cap-matched control. Validated in four independent samples (muni & corp, IS & OOS each). 2026-08 audit: the issuer cap previously keyed on an id prefix and never bound; it now uses real issuers (book 573→336 OOS trades, returns intact). OOS win rates are flattered by right-censored final-year holds and universe survivorship — the durable excess estimate is +2.5–3.6%; live recovery exits should trigger on the prior day's mid (≈−0.4 to −0.7pp/trade). Known failure mode — sustained rate selloffs

Mean-reversion is short a rate shock. In 2022 the whole market repriced down and dislocations did not revert (−1.99% excess). Size accordingly; an optional MUB-trend overlay mitigates it.
