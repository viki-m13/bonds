GRANITE-XL

Dislocation-reversion in individual U.S. corporate bonds, refined through four research rounds, three disclosed out-of-sample batches, ~19 killed strategy families, and a bidirectional muni↔corporate transfer test. Every number below is at honest daily marks (positions re-marked at actual mid prints, monthly Sharpe vs T-bill) with fills at the actual customer ask (buy) and bid (sell).

The five rules

Dislocation entry. Buy when a bond's customer-ask prints ≥3 points below its trailing 60-day median clean price and it passes a trailing-liquidity gate (≥8 active days / 90). Parameters transferred verbatim from the muni strategy — zero corporate fitting.
Short duration only (≤5y). Short bonds must pull to par, so dislocations revert; long bonds are rates bets. OOS-validated; repairs the 2008 GFC.
Limit entry — never chase. Accept the fill only if the ask ≤ the latest prior mid + 0.25. An implicit depth filter (accepted fills average 4.3 pts deep); monotone in the cap; transfers in reverse to munis.
One position per issuer; size ∝ depth (weight = depth/3, capped 0.5–2×). Issuer diversification beats raw breadth.
Recovery exit. Sell at the first bid ≥21 days in once the day's mid recovers to the entry-day median; 455-day hard stop. Harvest the reversion, recycle the capital (holds ~380→266 days).

Why it works — the response-curve atlas

Forward returns (mid + accrual) after each event type, in excess of a matched baseline on the same universe. Three laws: reversion is monotone in depth and slow (the payoff lives at 63–365 days — short-horizon trading dies to the bid-ask toll); volume alone is noise; and momentum is inverted (bonds at their 250-day high underperform — the par ceiling caps upside). Execution & live replication

Already inside every backtest number: fills pay the full printed bid–ask (buy at the customer-ask VWAP, sell at the customer-bid VWAP); no fill unless the required side actually printed (no phantom liquidity, 7-day fill window or no trade); entry is the day after the signal (no latency assumption); dead bonds exit at their last real bid. What is not automatic is competition for the print and size — modeled below.

Live-desk checklist — what a replication must handle

    Honest limits

    What this strategy is

    • One real phenomenon — forced-seller dislocations in short-dated credit revert — executed with discipline.
    • OOS CAGR +17.1% with monthly Sharpe ~1.0 at the panel's yield-proxy carry; on recovered real coupons the same fills give OOS +12.6–14.2% / Sharpe(m) 0.84–0.86 (the range is the windowing convention: slicing the full run by entry date vs re-running the OOS window standalone), annual-frequency Sharpe ~0.6 (2026-08 audit — see XL_AUDIT.md). Excess robust in every era and in the most-liquid quartile (capacity-viable).
    • Validated by kill-tested search: ~19 strategy families died on pre-registered gates around it.

    What it is not

    • Not market-neutral: long credit beta throughout; honest maxDD ~−34% in systemic crises. Demonstrated irreducible: nine downside-protection mechanisms (floors, stops, sibling filters, ML tail-avoidance, exit loosening, deployment pacing) all failed pre-registered gates — every one either sold the crisis returns or left the marks untouched. Size the book so the drawdown is tolerable; that is the price of the CAGR.
    • Not Sharpe 3: without CDS/futures hedging, shorting, or leverage, Sharpe ~1.0–1.2 is this instrument set's frontier — demonstrated, not assumed.
    • Backtest fills assume the patient trader captures the posted customer ask/bid (OSBAP daily VWAP), not guaranteed executable ticks.
