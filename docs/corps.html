1Executive summary

The same dealer-market inefficiency that rewards patient buyers of dislocated municipal bonds is present — and independently harvestable — in individual corporate bonds. Buying a bond when it prints several points below its own recent level (a forced-seller dislocation) and holding to reversion earns a statistically significant excess return over random entry in the same bonds, across a full 22-year sample that spans the 2008 GFC and the 2020 COVID shock.

What a manager is buying bias-audited

A repeatable source of +2.1% per-trade excess over a matched control (p<0.001) in a $10-trillion market, validated on the full survivorship-complete universe (every bond, defaulters included), with parameters transferred verbatim from the muni strategy — zero corporate-specific fitting. Attempts to "improve" it with regime/credit overlays were rejected for failing out-of-sample.

2Opportunity & microstructure

US corporates are a ~$10-trillion, over-the-counter dealer market of hundreds of thousands of CUSIPs. Like munis — but with wider spreads and richer credit dispersion — most bonds trade intermittently and customers cross a bid–ask spread on every trade. Every transaction is reported to FINRA (TRACE). Short-horizon timing loses to the spread; a patient buyer of idiosyncratic dislocations is paid a reversion premium for absorbing forced selling (fund redemptions, downgrades, tax-loss and index-exclusion selling).

Fig 1 — Mean customer round-trip return (buy ask → sell bid) by holding horizon, unconditional across all bond-days. Break-even ~4–6 months; the strategy holds ~1 year.

3Data & survivorship

Source: Open Source Bond Asset Pricing (Dickerson et al.) — a free, processed daily corporate-bond panel built from TRACE, with clean price, bid, ask, yield and credit spread. The bid/ask lets us model execution faithfully (buy at the ask, sell at the bid).

Survivorship-complete

The panel is the full TRACE tape, including bonds that default, get called or go quiet. 70% of bonds stop trading before the sample end and are retained with their final prices; 8% ever crash below 50; and 7–14% of strategy positions exit "stale" — the bond stopped trading mid-hold — taking the realized loss. The edge is measured after those losses.

4Methodology — the honesty harness

  • Fills at real bid/ask. Buys fill at the day's ask, sells at the bid — the spread is paid, never assumed away.
  • Point-in-time, no look-ahead. Trailing median is lagged one day; the liquidity gate counts only trailing dates; entry is the first ask strictly after the signal; exits use only prices at/before exit.
  • Matched random-entry control. Every result is scored vs random entries in the same bonds, same window, same exit and cost model — isolating timing skill. Significance is a 2,000-sample cluster bootstrap.
  • Clean IS/OOS split. Entries ≤2015 in-sample; 2016–2025 out-of-sample; both reported.
  • Full universe. All 55,545 bonds with ≥20 trading days (99.9% of bond-days) — no survivor pre-selection.

5The signal

GRANITE entry rule

Enter when a bond's ask prints ≥3 points below its trailing 60-day median clean price and it clears the liquidity gate. Fill at the ask. Exit at the bid after a ~1-year hold. The rule is the muni KEYSTONE specification applied unchanged — a deliberate out-of-sample transfer test across asset classes.

8Selectivity — a focused short-duration book

The full-universe book carries ~3,100 concurrent positions — more than a real desk would run. Can a much smaller, higher-conviction book keep, or beat, the edge? We tested point-in-time filters on credit quality (entry-day credit spread) and duration (years to maturity), each baked into the eligibility gate so the matched control trades the same selected subset. The answer is decisive — and it overturns the intuitive guess that "trade only the safest bonds."

Credit quality — filtering to high grade backfires

Top-IG dislocations have no out-of-sample timing edge and even lost money outright (the 2022 rate selloff crushed long high-grade). The reversion alpha lives in crossover/HY names; only the very deepest distress (spread >5%) is worth trimming, and only as a tail-risk knob.

Duration — the edge concentrates in short bonds

Years to maturityOOS tradesWinMean/tradeExcess

Short-dated (≤5y) dislocations must pull to par, so they revert — OOS excess +3.5%, double the full book, on ~30% of the trades. Long bonds (>12y) show zero OOS edge: their price is driven by rates, not idiosyncratic reversion.

The focused operating point (≤5y) vs the full book

Short-dated selection is not an overfit overlay: it is monotone across the maturity spectrum, economically structural (pull-to-par), improves both IS and OOS, and — unlike the rejected regime gate in §9 — repairs the 2008 GFC out-of-sample rather than curve-fitting it. Fewer names, higher conviction, and the base book's one weak regime is gone.

8.5Refinements & the honest risk picture (second research round)

A second research program — six agent "trading lenses" proposing 24 specs, 13 strategy families screened in-sample with pre-registered kill gates, survivors given exactly one out-of-sample look — searched for a higher-Sharpe construction and stress-tested this strategy's accounting. Three results matter:

1 · Honest mark-to-market risk (correction)

The equity-curve drawdowns above use linear intra-trade attribution, which smooths the path. Re-marking every position daily at its actual mid prints (stale marks flat, monthly Sharpe vs T-bill):

Total return and CAGR are unaffected — they were always realized from bid/ask fills. Real risk is ~2× the smoothed path; risk-adjusted return is ordinary, not exceptional.

2 · GRANITE-CL — the validated refinement

On the issuer-capped ≤5y book, accept the fill only if the ask ≤ latest prior mid + 0.25 — never chase a bounced price (the control faces the same fill cap). One frozen parameter; the cap is an implicit depth filter, and depth was monotone from the first sweep.

Diagnostics: cap monotone, excess positive in all 6 eras (incl. GFC +4.66% and 2021–23 +1.37%), strongest in the most liquid quartile.

Same entries, two more frozen rules: size ∝ dislocation depth (capped 0.5–2×) and sell at the first bid ≥21d in once the day's mid recovers to the entry-day median (455d stop). Third one-shot OOS batch, paired on identical entries:

2026-08 audit: those rows accrue carry at the panel's median-yield proxy; re-priced on recovered real coupons the same fills give IS +17.8%/1.09, OOS +12.6–14.2%/0.84–0.86 (windowing convention), full +14.7%/0.93. Implementable live-protocol replays land at OOS +11–14% CAGR — budget those, not the headline. See XL_AUDIT.md.

3 · The negative result, documented

The search's target — Sharpe ≈3 / CAGR ≥10% — was not met, with the mechanism demonstrated rather than asserted: the bid-ask toll is paid twice per round trip (it killed every short-horizon sleeve, including one that was significant and monotone in-sample yet still died out-of-sample); all surviving sleeves are long the same credit-beta factor (OOS correlations 0.69–0.85, so combination cannot lift Sharpe); vol-targeting trades CAGR for Sharpe without leverage. Clean kills: fire-sale reversal, spread-widening reversion, carry ladders, issuer relative-value, momentum, fallen-angel proxy, new-issue concession, index-exclusion and hold-to-redemption constructions, vol-managed sizing, calendar effects. The refinement path lifted the frontier to Sharpe ~1.0–1.2 at CAGR ~17% OOS (GRANITE-XL) — the CAGR-10 target is met out-of-sample; Sharpe 3 is not, because every position remains long the single credit-beta factor through a −34% crisis drawdown, and tripling Sharpe requires hedging instruments (CDS, futures, repo leverage) this dataset does not contain. 10Robust levers & limitations

Robust operating levers (OOS-validated)

Deeper thresholds concentrate the alpha (fewer, higher-conviction trades); a dynamic recovery-exit cuts average holding ~35% at similar annualized return.

Limitations (disclosed)

  • Carry proxied by median yield (nets out of the control excess).
  • Equity-curve drawdown smoothed by linear attribution; returns are realized.
  • Capacity: full-universe number includes illiquid names; a live book tiers by liquidity.
  • Prices are OSBAP cleaned daily VWAP + bid/ask, not raw ticks.
  • Systemic crises (GFC) are the full book's weakest regime; the focused ≤5y book (§8) repairs it out-of-sample, leaving the 2021–23 rate selloff as the soft spot.
