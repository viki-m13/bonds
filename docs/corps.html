1Executive summary

The same dealer-market inefficiency that rewards patient buyers of dislocated municipal bonds is present — and independently harvestable — in individual corporate bonds. Buying a bond when it prints several points below its own recent level (a forced-seller dislocation) and holding to reversion earns a statistically significant excess return over random entry in the same bonds, across a full 22-year sample that spans the 2008 GFC and the 2020 COVID shock.

What a manager is buying bias-audited

A repeatable source of +2.1% per-trade excess over a matched control (p<0.001) in a $10-trillion market, validated on the full survivorship-complete universe (every bond, defaulters included), with parameters transferred verbatim from the muni strategy — zero corporate-specific fitting. Attempts to "improve" it with regime/credit overlays were rejected for failing out-of-sample.

2Opportunity & microstructure

US corporates are a ~$10-trillion, over-the-counter dealer market of hundreds of thousands of CUSIPs. Like munis — but with wider spreads and richer credit dispersion — most bonds trade intermittently and customers cross a bid–ask spread on every trade. Every transaction is reported to FINRA (TRACE). Short-horizon timing loses to the spread; a patient buyer of idiosyncratic dislocations is paid a reversion premium for absorbing forced selling (fund redemptions, downgrades, tax-loss and index-exclusion selling).

Fig 1 — Mean customer round-trip return (buy ask → sell bid) by holding horizon, unconditional across all bond-days. Break-even ~4–6 months; the strategy holds ~1 year.

3Data & survivorship

Source: Open Source Bond Asset Pricing (Dickerson et al.) — a free, processed daily corporate-bond panel built from TRACE, with clean price, bid, ask, yield and credit spread. The bid/ask lets us model execution faithfully (buy at the ask, sell at the bid).

Survivorship-complete

The panel is the full TRACE tape, including bonds that default, get called or go quiet. 70% of bonds stop trading before the sample end and are retained with their final prices; 8% ever crash below 50; and 7–14% of strategy positions exit "stale" — the bond stopped trading mid-hold — taking the realized loss. The edge is measured after those losses.

4Methodology — the honesty harness

  • Fills at real bid/ask. Buys fill at the day's ask, sells at the bid — the spread is paid, never assumed away.
  • Point-in-time, no look-ahead. Trailing median is lagged one day; the liquidity gate counts only trailing dates; entry is the first ask strictly after the signal; exits use only prices at/before exit.
  • Matched random-entry control. Every result is scored vs random entries in the same bonds, same window, same exit and cost model — isolating timing skill. Significance is a 2,000-sample cluster bootstrap.
  • Clean IS/OOS split. Entries ≤2015 in-sample; 2016–2025 out-of-sample; both reported.
  • Full universe. All 55,545 bonds with ≥20 trading days (99.9% of bond-days) — no survivor pre-selection.

5The signal

GRANITE entry rule

Enter when a bond's ask prints ≥3 points below its trailing 60-day median clean price and it clears the liquidity gate. Fill at the ask. Exit at the bid after a ~1-year hold. The rule is the muni KEYSTONE specification applied unchanged — a deliberate out-of-sample transfer test across asset classes.

9Robust levers & limitations

Robust operating levers (OOS-validated)

Deeper thresholds concentrate the alpha (fewer, higher-conviction trades); a dynamic recovery-exit cuts average holding ~35% at similar annualized return.

Limitations (disclosed)

  • Carry proxied by median yield (nets out of the control excess).
  • Equity-curve drawdown smoothed by linear attribution; returns are realized.
  • Capacity: full-universe number includes illiquid names; a live book tiers by liquidity.
  • Prices are OSBAP cleaned daily VWAP + bid/ask, not raw ticks.
  • Systemic crises (GFC) are the weakest regime — mean-reversion is short a tail.
